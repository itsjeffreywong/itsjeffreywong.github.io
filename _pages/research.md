My research examines how health and community services can support people's social needs, with a particular focus on older adults. I bring experience in evidence synthesis, community-engaged research, knowledge translation, and social prescribing practice.

## Social prescribing and digital health

At Simon Fraser University's HEAL Lab, I contribute to the provincial scale-up and evaluation of social prescribing for older adults in British Columbia. My graduate research focuses on digital referral and information-sharing processes between primary care and community-based services.

I am interested in what happens after a referral: how people connect with support, how providers exchange information, and how these processes can fit into everyday practice.

## Aging, social connection, and community supports

My previous work includes a scoping review of community-based social supports for 2S/LGBTQ+ older adults in Canada, developed in collaboration with community partners. I have also contributed to research on loneliness and social isolation among sexual and gender minority older adults, including secondary analysis of the Canadian Social Connection Survey.

In community practice at Langley Senior Resources Society, I worked with older adults to develop wellness plans and connect with local resources. I also helped develop a program evaluation framework and follow-up survey for social prescribing referrals from health services to the community.

## Technology and care in later life

I have contributed to studies of socially assistive robots, virtual reality co-design with Indigenous peoples, and virtual seated dance in long-term care. These projects inform my interest in how technology can support meaningful connections and respond to the priorities of people receiving care.

## Research communication

I co-lead the Canadian Social Prescribing Student Collective and have contributed to knowledge translation through reports, presentations, and public writing. My student blog post, [Is social prescribing just another overhyped health policy trend?](https://cspsc.ca/is-social-prescribing-just-another-overhyped-health-policy-trend/), reflects on the promise and implementation of social prescribing.
